Why are we losing?

A revenue chief has 400 closed deals, three competing explanations and a CRM she does not trust. This is the two-page brief an AI agent wrote for her board after reading every call. Each claim in it opens the buyer quotes and the deals behind it.

A fictional sample client. Lanternmere HR, its people, its competitors and all 400 deals are invented: synthetic deals with planted patterns, written for this project. The model calls that read them are real. Recovering planted patterns tests the method. It does not prove the agent would be as accurate on real deals.

How to read it: text marked like this is a claim. Click one to see its evidence.

The sample client’s starting point

The question

From: Annika Lindqvist, Chief Revenue Officer (fictional) · To: Revenue Operations · Subject: Why are we losing?

Our win rate has gone from 28% to 19% in four quarters. In last week’s exec meeting I heard three explanations and no evidence.

Sales says we are too expensive. Product says we are missing features. Marketing says the broker channel sends us weak leads.

The CRM loss reason says “Price” on 61% of losses. I don’t believe it, because it is the first option in the dropdown.

We record every call. The answers are in the transcripts, and nobody has time to read 400 deals. I need a brief for the board meeting: what is causing the losses, by segment, with quotes I can read aloud, and what each team should do about it.

Method note

How it was tested

The deals were generated in one step with planted patterns and an answer key. The analysis ran in a separate step and never saw the key. Afterwards the brief was scored against it. These are all seven checks, with counts.

How a deal is coded

  1. Claude Haiku pulled the buyers’ reason phrases from a sample of 40 losses. Claude Sonnet proposed seven reason categories from those phrases.
  2. Claude Sonnet read each lost deal’s call notes and email, and nothing else: no CRM reason, no segment. It picked one main reason, an optional second one, a confidence level and one or two word-for-word quotes.
  3. Code checked every quote against the source text. A quote that is not an exact copy is removed, along with the claim it supported.
  4. Code counted everything. A second, separate piece of code recomputed every figure in the brief from the coded table.
